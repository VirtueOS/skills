# Derive art direction from the project

Read this when creating or substantially revising an asset's appearance. Locate the project's art guide, approved concepts, and neighboring assets through its documentation index. Reuse the visual decisions already supplied; clarify only choices that remain unresolved and affect the result.

## Form and construction

Extract the silhouette, proportions, construction, detail density, symmetry, and surface treatment from the approved references. Preserve deliberate stylization. Judge significant geometry from the intended viewing distance; use texture or material detail where it gives the required result without unnecessary geometry.

Check attachments, openings, moving-part clearances, and joins according to the concept and intended use. Inspect modular boundaries against real neighboring assets. Derive dimensions and pivots from the consuming scene or explicit specification rather than another asset's history.

## Readability and materials

Use the actual presentation camera and lighting to determine which surfaces and details need emphasis. Preserve the project's established visual language for interaction, variants, and states. Decorative elements should not accidentally imply behavior outside the brief.

Choose realistic, stylized, painterly, flat, or other surface treatment from the task's references. Match the requested wear, texture density, and material contrast. Inspect UV seams, projection, repetition, and texture scale on the model, including dark and reflective regions. Neutral review lighting helps distinguish material decisions from presentation effects.

## Project knowledge

Record new family-specific decisions in the owning project page with links to the actual resource and approved concept. Treat historical alternatives and rejected variants as context, not active requirements. Keep project palettes, reference images, asset names, and gameplay color meanings outside this reusable skill.
