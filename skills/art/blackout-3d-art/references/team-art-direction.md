# Blackout Protocol art direction

Bundled fallback requirements from the supplied team art package. Use the game's current art documentation and approved concepts first. Record future asset decisions in project-owned documentation so a shared toolkit does not become a second project configuration store.

## Form and readability

Use stylized industrial sci-fi with expressive proportions, readable construction, bevels, recessed panels, frames, and dark gaps. Aim between primitive blockouts and overloaded technical objects. Significant thickness, handles, fasteners, lids, tools, and cables belong in geometry; small surface wear belongs in textures/material maps. Attachments need visible support, holes remain open, and parts should fit without unintended intersections or coplanar surfaces.

The game often uses a high camera, so develop top surfaces and keep tools recognizable and contrasting at gameplay distance. New Martian props should not have predominantly white tops unless the approved concept requires it. Environmental decoration should read as decoration; interactive color and indicators follow the object's actual function.

## Color

Use cold white/light gray panels with graphite and near-black mechanical areas. Avoid large pure-white fills and beige/cream drift. Color whole logical parts rather than arbitrary polygons. Blue, green, and cold violet are restrained local accents, not a tint over the whole object. Yellow follows the object's purpose or approved variant, such as power equipment or yellow doors. Martian terracotta stripes are small and muted. Do not use every accent on every asset.

Preserve the meaning of interactive colors, including broad blue oxygen zones and yellow power markings. For an existing floor or wall, the actual base tile determines its exact tone; bundled palette images are not permission to recolor it.

## Painterly surfaces

Use visible large and medium brushwork, inspired by the supplied Disco Elysium texture direction, with moderate contrast and related shades within each material. Cold light materials may contain subtle warm/cold variation without turning cream. Dark materials need readable texture too. Place wear, chips, dirt, and lighter edges where they explain construction and use.

Avoid fine visual noise, uniform repeated spots, arbitrary white streaks, dominant oversized strokes, and flat fills replacing the texture. Fix stretched or seamed UVs through unwrap/projection changes. Check brush scale, grille tiling, and repetition on the model. Neutral review lighting should preserve texture visibility; dramatic lighting is a separate presentation choice.
